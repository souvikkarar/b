Buying a first guitar is exciting — and a little confusing. Walk into any music shop and you will find dozens of shapes, sizes and price tags. The good news is that you do not need an expensive instrument to start well. You need one that is comfortable, stays in tune and makes you want to pick it up every day.

## Acoustic or electric?

Both are perfectly good first instruments. The right choice depends mostly on the music you want to play.

- **Acoustic guitars** need no amplifier, which makes them simple to practise anywhere. They suit singer-songwriter music, folk, pop and Bollywood accompaniment. The strings are a little heavier, so fingertips take a few weeks to toughen up.
- **Electric guitars** have lighter strings and thinner necks, which many beginners find easier to press. They suit rock, blues and lead playing, but you will also need a small practice amplifier and cable.

If you are unsure, think about the songs you hum most often. Then choose the guitar that plays them.

## Getting the size right

Size matters most for children. A guitar that is too big makes it hard to reach the chords and leads to tension in the shoulder and wrist.

- Younger children often start on a **half-size or three-quarter-size** guitar.
- Teenagers and adults usually play a **full-size** guitar.
- Smaller adults may find a compact or "parlour" acoustic more comfortable.

When in doubt, sit with the guitar on your knee and check that your strumming arm rests naturally over the body and your other hand can reach the first fret without stretching.

## Nylon or steel strings?

Nylon strings (classical guitars) are softer on the fingers and produce a warm, mellow sound. Steel strings (acoustic guitars) are brighter and louder. Young children often start more happily on nylon strings, while most older beginners choose steel strings for pop and acoustic music.

## What to check before you buy

1. **Action** — the height of the strings above the fretboard. Lower action is easier to play.
2. **Tuning stability** — the guitar should stay in tune for a reasonable time after tuning.
3. **No buzzing** — play each string open and on a few frets. Persistent buzzing is a warning sign.
4. **Comfort** — hold it for a few minutes. If it feels awkward now, it will feel awkward after an hour of practice.

## A sensible budget

You do not need a premium instrument to start. Aim for a reputable beginner model rather than the cheapest option available — very cheap guitars are often hard to play, which is discouraging. Keep a little budget aside for a tuner, spare strings and a case.

## Ask before you buy

Every learner is different. If you are planning to start [guitar classes](/courses/guitar/), tell your teacher about the music you like and your budget before buying — they can help you avoid a costly mistake and choose an instrument that grows with you.
